Two Reasons SES and Reading Go Together

Within schools
Among children in the same school, those from higher-SES families tend to score higher.
Between schools
Higher-SES families tend to attend different schools, and those schools differ: teachers, resources, neighborhoods, classmates.
Ordinary regression mixes the two into one number. Fixed effects looks only at the first.

Fixed Effects in One Picture

Fixed effects compares each child only with children in the same school.

  4. The same student in different years

Three Ways to Estimate the Same Relationship

Part of the overall relationship comes from which schools children attend. Fixed effects sets that part aside.

What the Fixed Effects Coefficient Means

Among children in the same school, those one unit apart on SES differ by about 4.9 points in spring reading, on average.
Everything children in a school share is held constant: teachers, curriculum, neighborhood, principal.
That holds whether or not those things were measured.

What Fixed Effects Cannot Estimate

Anything that is the same for every child in a school: public or private, school size, location, a school-wide program.
areg x2rscalk1 x12sesl private, absorb(s2_id)

   x2rscalk1 | Coefficient  Std. err.      t    P>|t|     [95% conf. interval]
-------------+----------------------------------------------------------------

     private |          0  (omitted)
       _cons |   50.14242   .0837171   598.95   0.000     49.97832    50.30651

Random Effects and Fixed Effects Compared

Random effects Fixed effects
Compares Children within and between schools Children within the same school only
School-level characteristics
(public or private, size) 		Can estimate them Cannot estimate them
Assumes What makes schools differ is unrelated to the predictors Nothing about how schools differ
Use when The question is about a school-level characteristic Unmeasured school differences may be tied to the predictor

The Hausman Test

Do the random effects and fixed effects coefficients differ by more than chance? Like every statistical test, it follows the same steps.
1
Null hypothesis: the two sets of coefficients do not differ systematically
2
Test statistic: a chi-square, computed by Stata
3
p-value
4
Decision: p below 0.05, the models disagree, use fixed effects. Otherwise random effects is fine.

The Hausman Test in Stata

xtset s2_id
xtreg x2rscalk1 x12sesl, fe    estimates store fe
xtreg x2rscalk1 x12sesl, re    estimates store re
hausman fe re 
             |       fe           re         Difference       Std. err.
-------------+----------------------------------------------------------------
     x12sesl |    4.929909     5.251916       -.3220064        .0626305

Test of H0: Difference in coefficients not systematic
    chi2(1) = 26.43
Prob > chi2 = 0.0000
p is below 0.05: the two models disagree about SES, so fixed effects is the better choice here.

Can Fixed Effects Support a Causal Claim?

So far, our models have described associations. In some circumstances, fixed effects is used to make causal claims.
Fixed effects holds constant everything the members of a group share, measured or not.
If group members share nearly everything that matters, the differences that remain within the group begin to look like a fair comparison.

Three Well-Known Examples

Siblings and Head Start
Compare siblings in the same family, one who attended Head Start and one who did not.

Held constant: parents, home, neighborhood.
Currie & Thomas (1995); Deming (2009)
Twins and schooling
Compare the earnings of identical twins with different amounts of schooling.

Held constant: family background and genes.
Ashenfelter & Krueger (1994)
One student, two teachers
Compare the same student in two subjects, one taught by a same-gender teacher and one not.

Held constant: everything about the student.
Dee (2007)
The remaining question: why did one sibling attend, or one twin stay in school longer?

When You Read a Study

When you read a study
Look for “school fixed effects,” “within-school comparison,” or “controlling for school.” Fixed effects are also used for districts, teachers, and years.

Ask: Who is being compared with whom? Which characteristics can the model no longer speak to?
